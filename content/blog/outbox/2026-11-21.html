What is ChatGPT Wallet?

ChatGPT Wallet is OpenAI's way of letting its agents pay. The agents are called dots, and OpenAI launched Wallet on 13 November 2026 with the claim that it is the first wallet that lets an agent pay without access to your actual card details while requiring a security check on every purchase. It was built with American Express, Chase, Mastercard and Visa.

The pitch is simple. You already ask ChatGPT what to buy. OpenAI says about 1.2 billion people use it each week, and many use it to research products and compare options. Wallet adds the last step, which is the checkout.

What's new compared with earlier ChatGPT shopping?

The agent can now finish the purchase itself. Digital Trends notes that dots, OpenAI's autonomous agents, arrived just over a month ago, and Wallet gives them a payment method.

OpenAI has tried checkout before. Digital Commerce 360 reports that Instant Checkout launched in September 2025 and that OpenAI shifted to purchases through merchant apps in March. Wallet works through a store's existing checkout instead of replacing it, so the retailer still owns pricing, fulfilment, returns and customer service.

How does a purchase work?

You add a card once. After that, the flow looks like this:

  1. You tell your dot what you want, and it suggests products.
  2. Before paying, you see the items, the total and the card it will use.
  3. You approve with a security check such as Face ID or a fingerprint scan, and that approval is logged as a record of what you allowed.
  4. The payment network issues a token, a stand-in with its own card number, security code and expiry date, and your real card is charged through it.

OpenAI says neither the store nor the agent gets your actual card details. TechCrunch adds that the merchant still receives what it needs to ship the order, such as your address and contact details. Your card's rewards and its dispute protections for things like a defective item stay in place, according to OpenAI.

I have not tried it, and I could not find an independent hands-on test yet. What I can tell you is what OpenAI and the outlets covering the launch describe, and that Digital Commerce 360 says OpenAI has not named any retailers that accept Wallet payments so far.

Pros

  • OpenAI says the agent and the store never get your real card details.
  • OpenAI says every purchase needs a security check from you, such as Face ID.
  • You keep the rewards and dispute protections your card already has.
  • Amex, Mastercard and Visa are partners, and Amex calls this its first agentic commerce integration.
  • You can go from idea to order inside one chat.

Cons

  • Access is narrow: US Pro subscribers aged 18 and over with eligible US-issued cards, and TechCrunch says a limited number of them at first.
  • OpenAI itself warns that some websites may block agent purchases and that risk systems may flag legitimate ones, which can mean declines or cancellations.
  • OpenAI says spending limits, categories and time windows are planned for later. Today you approve each buy by hand.
  • As of 16 November, per Digital Commerce 360, OpenAI had not named any stores that accept Wallet.
  • TechCrunch points to Amazon, the largest retailer to ban AI agents, and The Next Web reports that United Airlines, Schwab and JPMorgan are also blocking agents in their own areas.

Who should try it, and who should skip it?

Try it if you are a US-based Pro subscriber who already uses a dot for research, who is comfortable approving every order, and who wants to see how agent checkout behaves while the stakes are small.

Skip it for now if you live outside the US, which includes readers in India, since OpenAI's announcement names only US availability. Skip it too if you want to hand an agent a budget and walk away, because that version does not exist yet. If you run an online shop, the more relevant item is OpenAI's Commerce Partner Playbook, which covers product feeds, letting signed agent requests through your firewall and talking to your payment provider about false declines.

Price and availability

Wallet is rolling out through dots and needs a ChatGPT Pro subscription. I could not confirm a separate Wallet fee. The Next Web, citing CNBC, says OpenAI takes no cut of purchases for now. No date has been given for wider access. OpenAI only says it will work with businesses, banks and payment networks to expand availability.

Is it safe to let an agent pay?

On paper the design is careful. OpenAI says the agent never gets your card details and that every charge needs your approval. The open question is how often agents pick the wrong thing. The Next Web reports that Visa tested 22 models as shoppers this month and they failed 42.8% of the time. That report does not say how Wallet itself would fare, but it is a reminder that shopping agents still get things wrong. I wrote about one of those moves in United's ban on AI agents.

Stores are the other weak point. OpenAI's own wording is that agentic commerce is still in its early days.

My take

I'd wait unless you are a US Pro user who is curious. The security design looks careful, mainly because OpenAI says the agent never gets your card details. But with no merchant list, no spending caps and several big companies already blocking agents, it reads as an early preview. If you run a shop, I'd read OpenAI's playbook before anything else. Everyone else can watch for spending limits and a named list of stores, and take a second look when both arrive.

Frequently asked questions

What is ChatGPT Wallet?

ChatGPT Wallet is a payment feature OpenAI launched on 13 November 2026. It lets OpenAI's dots agents pay for purchases on your behalf, using a payment token instead of your real card number, and it asks you to approve each purchase with a security check such as Face ID or a fingerprint scan first.

Can ChatGPT see my credit card number?

OpenAI says the business and the agent do not receive your actual card details. After you approve a purchase, the payment network issues a token that stands in for your card, with its own number, security code and expiry date, and your card is charged without sharing the real details.

Who can use ChatGPT Wallet right now?

Wallet starts with ChatGPT Pro subscribers aged 18 and over in the United States who hold eligible US-issued cards. TechCrunch reports it is a limited number of subscribers at first, and OpenAI says it will expand access later after learning from early use with businesses, banks and payment networks.

Can I set a spending limit on ChatGPT Wallet?

Not yet. OpenAI says it expects to let users set spending limits, purchase categories or time periods over time, so agents can handle pre-approved purchases. For now, you review the items, total and card for each purchase and approve it yourself with a biometric check.

Will every online store accept ChatGPT Wallet payments?

No. OpenAI warns that some websites may block agent purchases without meaning to, and that fraud systems can flag legitimate agent orders. It published a Commerce Partner Playbook to help businesses, and Digital Commerce 360 says it has not named specific retailers that accept Wallet.
