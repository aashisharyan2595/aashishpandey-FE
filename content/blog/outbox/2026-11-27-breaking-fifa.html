Quick answer. FIFA confirmed on 26 November 2026 that criminals got into a limited set of its personal and business data. A hacker group appeared to take credit and is linked to a claim of over 12 gigabytes; FIFA didn't say how many people are affected.

What happened

On Thursday, 26 November 2026, FIFA, the body that runs world football, said criminals had broken into its systems. A spokesperson told Al Jazeera the attack led to "unauthorised access to a limited set of personal and business information." FIFA says it is working with the relevant authorities, including law enforcement.

FIFA also condemned the unlawful acquisition and publication of personal data in strong terms and said it can have significant consequences for the people concerned, The Hill reports.

What the hackers claim

Last week, a group that calls itself the Handsome Boy Hacking Club shared an X screenshot of what it said were FIFA files, SecurityWeek reports. Within three days it appeared to own the hack, answering a post about more than 12 gigabytes of leaked internal documents.

Per UPI via Yahoo Sports, the group says the files cover ID and immigration papers, executive details, finances and legal records. That's the hackers' word, and FIFA's statement doesn't confirm those details.

What sources close to FIFA say

A person with knowledge of the matter told Reuters the damage covers under 0.1% of FIFA's data, according to Fox Business. The abandoned stake-sale plan, which aimed to raise up to $4.2 billion, was reportedly left alone.

On the how: a FIFA source told The Athletic the intruder pretended to be staff, not that a system failed. If that's right, a person was fooled, not a firewall. In my view, that's the kind of attack a small business can defend against with a call-back habit and a rule that nobody sends documents just because someone sounds urgent.

What we don't know yet

FIFA didn't say how many people or groups are affected, when it happened, or what the "business information" is. Its mention of "publication" doesn't say whether any data is online. None of the sources I read, apart from the hackers' account, says identity documents were taken.

Watch for two things: whether FIFA starts telling individuals, and whether leaked files appear in a way outsiders can check.

My take

This is a confirmed breach of unknown size. The small percentage is encouraging, but it came from a source, not from FIFA. Until FIFA says what left the building, I'd read the hackers' list as unproven and FIFA's wording as vague, and stay wary of any message that mentions the organisation.

Frequently asked questions

What did FIFA confirm about the cyberattack?

On 26 November 2026 FIFA confirmed a criminal cyberattack that gave intruders access to a limited set of personal and business information. It condemned the unlawful acquisition and publication of personal data and said it is cooperating with authorities, law enforcement included. It gave no figures.

Who claims to be behind the FIFA hack?

A group calling itself the Handsome Boy Hacking Club shared a screenshot of alleged FIFA files on X, then appeared to claim the hack days later. Outlets report this as the group's claim. FIFA didn't name anyone, and the reports I read don't verify the group's account.

How did the attackers get into FIFA's systems?

A FIFA source told The Athletic the attacker posed as staff instead of exploiting a technical flaw. Al Jazeera and The Hill both relay it. The reports give no technical details, so how the impersonation worked and how long the access lasted aren't known.

Were the World Cup stake sale files stolen?

Reports say no. A person with knowledge of the matter told Reuters that files from the abandoned FIFA Forward Enterprise plan weren't reached, and that under 0.1% of FIFA's data was affected. Those figures come from a source, though, not from FIFA itself.
