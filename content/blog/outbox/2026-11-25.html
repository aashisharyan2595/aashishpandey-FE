Quick answer. The RBI merged its rules for sending cash to a bank account into one document on 11 November 2026 and, by most reports, kept the limits: ₹50,000 at a branch, ₹5,000 per transfer and ₹25,000 a month through agents and ATMs, and ₹10,000 per payout. The text also lists safeguards banks must run.

The Reserve Bank of India has put its rules for sending cash to someone else's bank account into one document. Business and news outlets report that the cash limits stay where they were. The RBI text sets out who can send how much, and what banks must monitor. Here are the four limits and the checks in the document.

What did the RBI actually issue?

On 11 November the RBI published the Domestic Money Transfer Directions, 2026. Its stated aim is to bring the older instructions together in one place. The document says the directions apply immediately and repeals two earlier circulars, one from 5 October 2011 and one from 24 July 2024.

They apply to commercial banks, rural and co-operative banks, and the payment operators that take part. Card-to-card transfers are left out and follow their own rules. The RBI says the framework exists for small transfers through formal channels, particularly for people such as migrant workers who lack access to them for want of proof of identity or address.

Which four limits matter?

Several outlets, including Outlook Money and Business Today, list the same numbers, and they match the RBI text.

  1. ₹50,000 at a bank branch. A walk-in customer can deposit cash at a branch and send up to ₹50,000 to a beneficiary's bank account through NEFT. Outlook Money notes this is also open to people who do not hold an account with that bank.
  2. ₹5,000 per transfer through agents and ATMs. Business correspondents, ATMs and other permitted channels allow up to ₹5,000 per transaction.
  3. ₹25,000 a month per sender. That is the cap on one remitter across those agent and ATM channels.
  4. ₹10,000 per payout, ₹25,000 a month per receiver. Someone without a bank account can collect cash that a bank customer sent them, up to ₹10,000 per transaction and ₹25,000 per beneficiary per month.

Channeliam says these limits were already part of the existing framework, and Free Press Journal says the RBI clarified the same. The RBI notification itself only says it consolidates the existing instructions. The Hans India, citing IANS, says the rules "largely consolidate existing instructions rather than introduce substantial changes to transaction limits".

What checks does the RBI text require?

The notification lists these safeguards. I can't tell from the sources which are new and which carry over from the older circulars:

  • The sender signs up with a verified mobile number and a self-certified ID document, as the KYC rules require.
  • Every transaction needs an additional factor of authentication.
  • The bank must put the sender's details in the IMPS or NEFT message and mark the transfer as a cash-based remittance.
  • For payouts, the sending bank must record the beneficiary's name and address.
  • Banks and non-bank providers must run velocity checks and send customers alerts about credits that arrive through these facilities.

In practice that means a sender registers once and confirms each transaction with a second factor. The text requires alerts about credits into accounts, so account holders on the receiving end should see them. It does not say what a receiver without an account gets.

What happens if something looks odd?

The directions say any unusual spurt in the number of credits into one account, or a group of accounts, "shall be investigated immediately". Anything suspicious has to be passed to the authorities. Business Today describes these steps as preventing misuse, and The Hans India says they are meant to prevent fraud and misuse. The RBI text itself does not name a particular scam, so I would not read more into it than that.

Transfers are meant to settle in real time or close to it, and banks settle with each other only through RBI-approved payment systems.

What about fees and complaints?

There is no fixed fee. Banks and non-banks may set "reasonable charges", and Business Today points out that the directions do not set one uniform fee. Ask the branch or agent what they charge before you hand over cash.

For complaints, the directions place grievances under the RBI's Integrated Ombudsman Scheme, 2026. Cash deposits must also follow the Income-Tax Act, 2025 provisions on cash deposits.

What don't we know yet?

The directions give no number for what counts as an "unusual spurt" in credits. They don't say how a customer will get the credit alert, by SMS, app or something else. And the coverage I read has no actual fee figures from any bank.

What to watch

Watch for bank notices that spell out their own charges, which the RBI left to them, and how they deliver credit alerts, which the text doesn't specify.

My take

Going by the reports, this looks like consolidation more than a new regime. If you send money home in cash, the numbers you already knew appear to hold, so I'd not change how I send it. I'd keep my ID and phone handy, ask about fees up front, and tell the bank about any credit I didn't expect.

This is a regulatory notice, so there is no left or right split to report. The sources are the RBI text and Indian business and news sites, which give the same figures.

Frequently asked questions

How much cash can a walk-in customer send to someone's bank account?

At a bank branch, a walk-in customer can send up to ₹50,000 in cash to a beneficiary's bank account through NEFT. Through business correspondents, ATMs and other permitted channels the limit is ₹5,000 per transaction and ₹25,000 per month for one sender.

How much cash can a person without a bank account collect?

A beneficiary without a bank account can collect up to ₹10,000 per transaction in cash, with a monthly ceiling of ₹25,000 per beneficiary. The money comes from a bank customer's account and is paid through an ATM, a business correspondent or another authorised channel.

Did the RBI change the limits in November 2026?

Not according to the reports. The RBI consolidated its earlier instructions into the Domestic Money Transfer Directions, 2026, issued on 11 November. Outlook Money and Channeliam report that the existing cash limits were retained, and The Hans India says the rules largely consolidate existing instructions. The text also lists safeguards and monitoring duties for banks.

What ID do I need to send cash through a bank or agent?

The sender must register with a verified mobile number and a self-certified officially valid document under the RBI's KYC rules. Every transaction also needs an additional factor of authentication, and the sender's details go into the IMPS or NEFT message.

Will I pay a fee for these transfers?

Possibly. The RBI lets banks and non-bank providers fix reasonable charges and does not set one uniform fee. The coverage gives no figures, so ask your branch or agent for the exact charge before you hand over cash.
