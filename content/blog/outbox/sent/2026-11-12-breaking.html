Quick answer. Jensen Huang resigned as Nvidia's president, CEO and board member on 12 November 2026, effective immediately. CFO Colette Kress is interim CEO while the board searches for a permanent replacement.

What happened

On Thursday, 12 November 2026, Nvidia's board announced that Jensen Huang has resigned as president, chief executive and a member of the board, with immediate effect. He co-founded the company in 1993 and ran it for 33 years.

Colette Kress, the chief financial officer since September 2013, steps in as interim CEO and keeps her finance job too. The board also named Harvey C. Jones, a former Synopsys chief executive, as Nvidia's first-ever board chair. A search for a permanent CEO is under way, and the board says it will look at people inside and outside the company.

What's confirmed and what isn't

The resignation, the interim CEO and the new chair come straight from Nvidia. So does the earnings date: third-quarter results are still due on Tuesday, 17 November.

The reasons are less tidy. Nvidia's statement gives none. News reports tie the exit to a week of pressure after Huang admitted on Sunday to an affair with a former executive at Megaspeed, a Singapore-based cloud company, whom US officials believe was working for Chinese intelligence, according to CNBC citing the Financial Times. Beijing denies she is a spy, Reuters reported. On Wednesday The New York Times reported a second relationship, with a senior Nvidia executive who reported to him. The board met that night to discuss his future, according to Al Jazeera and Reuters.

The spying claims come from US officials, as reported by the Financial Times and Reuters. Huang told President Trump he had "absolutely no idea" she was an intelligence operative, Al Jazeera reported. Earlier this month Nvidia's board started its own probe, led by independent directors with outside lawyers.

Huang has now agreed to answer questions from Congress by Thanksgiving, House Foreign Affairs chair Brian Mast said, according to Bloomberg and NBC News as cited by TechCrunch. The session will be behind closed doors.

Why it matters beyond Nvidia

Nvidia supplies most of the chips powering advanced AI, and it's worth roughly $4 trillion. Its shares fell more than 10% in early trading before recovering to about 4% down, CNBC said. Bernstein cut its price target from $400 to $200.

There was no succession plan in place, according to TechCrunch, and that may be the bigger question for anyone who buys AI compute. Huang personally steered deals with cloud providers, AI labs and suppliers, and he pushed Washington to ease limits on chip sales to China. Bernstein's Stacy Rasgon told CNBC it's unclear who could be the visionary to lead Nvidia next. Others, like a Boston College associate dean quoted by Al Jazeera, call it a "speed bump" for a company in a strong position.

What to watch

Tuesday's earnings call will be Kress's first test as interim CEO. Watch for any change in guidance, any word on the CEO search, and whether export policy toward China shifts now that Huang is no longer lobbying for it.

My take

For most teams in India and elsewhere, I don't expect anything to change this week, since Nvidia says customer commitments continue as planned. I'd hold off on any big hardware or vendor decision until after Tuesday's call and the first signal on who leads Nvidia next.

Frequently asked questions

Why did Jensen Huang resign from Nvidia?

Nvidia's statement does not give a reason. News outlets including Reuters and CNBC link it to reports that Huang had an affair with a businesswoman US officials suspect of spying for China, and a second relationship with a senior Nvidia executive who reported to him. Beijing denies she is a spy, and Huang told President Trump he had no idea.

Who is running Nvidia now?

Colette Kress, Nvidia's chief financial officer since 2013, is interim CEO and keeps her CFO role. Harvey C. Jones, a former Synopsys chief executive, is the company's first-ever board chair. The board is searching for a permanent CEO and says it will consider both internal and external candidates.

Will this affect AI chip supply or GPU prices?

Nvidia says it expects operations, customer commitments and its product roadmap to continue without interruption, so there is no sign of a near-term supply change. The bigger questions are longer term: who sets strategy next, and whether US export policy toward China shifts without Huang pushing to ease limits on chip sales there.

When will we hear more from Nvidia?

Nvidia is still due to report third-quarter fiscal 2027 results on Tuesday, 17 November 2026, with an earnings call at 2 p.m. Pacific time. That call will be Colette Kress's first as interim CEO, and analysts will be listening for guidance and any update on the search for a permanent chief executive.
