Quick answer. Grand Theft Auto VI went on sale on 19 November 2026 for PS5 and Xbox Series X|S. Sensor Tower estimates about $1.84 billion in sales so far, but that is a third-party figure that counts pre-orders, and Take-Two hasn't confirmed it.

What happened

Grand Theft Auto VI went on sale on Thursday, 19 November 2026, on PlayStation 5 and Xbox Series X|S. It is the first main GTA since Grand Theft Auto V in 2013, according to WAFB. The Standard Edition costs $79.99 and the Ultimate Edition $99.99. There is no PC version and no PC date yet.

Rockstar's own announcement, posted by parent company Take-Two, calls it the highest rated game in history. Rockstar founder Sam Houser says the game "represents everything we have been working towards since we first started making games".

The headline number is money. CNBC reports that about 19.1 million copies had sold by Thursday morning, roughly $1.84 billion, according to Sensor Tower.

Is the $1.84 billion real?

It's an estimate, and it needs a careful read. It comes from Sensor Tower, a market research firm, not from Take-Two. Fox 5 DC says the company hasn't released official sales figures. Its own numbers could arrive as early as Friday.

The figure also isn't one day of sales. It includes months of pre-orders. Al Jazeera reports pre-orders had passed an estimated 15.8 million units, worth $1.54 billion, as of 17 November. By Fox 5's account, Sensor Tower's tracker showed about 17.1 million units, worth an estimated $1.65 billion, at noon on 18 November. So most of the total was already banked before anyone pressed start.

That matters when you compare it with older records. GTA V sold 11.21 million copies and made $815.7 million in its first 24 hours in 2013, a mark Guinness World Records still lists, Fox 5 says. By that yardstick GTA VI is about 2.25 times bigger. The catch: a launch this pre-order-heavy can flatten faster afterwards, as some analysts quoted by Fox 5 point out. Fox 5 also reports that about 75% of the estimated copies are for PlayStation and the rest for Xbox.

Why it matters beyond games

People didn't only buy GTA VI, they watched it. Streams Charts counted 125.5 million hours of GTA VI content across Twitch, YouTube and Kick in the first 24 hours, according to CNBC. CNBC also describes a side economy of clip editors and small channels, and says the earnings claims it cites couldn't be independently verified.

Then there's work. A GameSquare survey of 1,000 full-time employed US gamers, reported by Fox News, found that 10% planned to skip work for the launch and another 30% were considering time off.

The 99 score and the bugs

GTA VI holds a Metacritic score of 99 from 147 critic reviews, per The National. Digital Trends notes that this ties The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time from 1998, so the label depends on how you count. GTA IV and GTA V scored 98 and 97. IGN's reviewer called it "the single most impressive video game I have ever played".

The National's list of drawbacks: clunky combat, a 30-frames-per-second cap and a slow opening. Digital Trends adds that early reports have the base PS5 holding 30 FPS most of the time, with dips into the mid-20s, while the PS5 Pro doesn't show the problem.

Players have also reported bugs. PC Games Hardware lists crashes, mission bugs and lost progress on both consoles, with PS5 error code CE-108255-1 turning up in the threads. It also says the reports don't show how many players are affected. Its own reviewers logged no crash in 65 hours of play, and Rockstar's support page sends console errors to Sony and Microsoft for now.

What it costs in India

Here's the part that matters most to readers in India. YourStory gives the PlayStation India prices as Rs 5,999 for the Standard Edition and Rs 7,499 for Ultimate. If you don't own a console, it puts the PS5 Slim Digital at Rs 64,990 on Amazon India (28 October), which makes the starter bill about Rs 70,989.

The boxed edition reportedly holds only a download code, not a disc, so the pricier disc-drive PS5 buys you nothing extra for this game. It also flags that the PS5 needs at least 212 GB of free space.

What we don't know yet

We don't have Take-Two's own sales numbers, we can't say how much came from launch day alone, and we don't know how common the crashes are. There's no PC date or online mode yet. Wedbush estimates $3 billion in sales and 30 million copies by year end, Fox 5 reports via The New York Times.

What to watch

Three things: Take-Two's official figures against Sensor Tower's, the first patch, and week-two sales once the pre-order wave is gone.

My take

I'd treat $1.84 billion as a strong signal, not a verdict. Most of it is pre-orders, and the real test is week three. If you're in India and don't own a console yet, I'd think hard before spending about Rs 70,000 on one game, however good the reviews are.
