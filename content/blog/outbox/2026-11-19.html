United bans AI agents from booking flights: what it means

Quick answer. On 11 November 2026 United Airlines changed its contract of carriage to ban AI agents from making, changing or servicing tickets, even if the passenger approves each step. United cites account security, unintended purchases and federal rules; analysts point to control of the sale.

What did United actually change?

United announced on Wednesday 11 November 2026 that it would limit what AI bots can do on its digital platforms, with data security as the reason. CNBC frames it as the newest pushback against agents that can plan and book trips for travellers. Skift says United is the first U.S. airline to state explicitly that it will block third-party AI agents.

The teeth are in the paperwork. One Mile at a Time reports that United updated its contract of carriage, the terms every passenger accepts when buying a ticket. The prohibited practices now include using AI agents to make, modify or service a ticket, or to buy any other service or product from the airline.

How broad is the rule?

Broader than the headline suggests. The contract defines an AI agent as any software or service that takes autonomous or semi-autonomous action to discover, search for, book, buy or manage United products, and it applies whether or not the passenger supervises or approves each step. The list includes assistants built into social platforms, AI-enabled browsers, standalone agents, and any third-party assistant that reaches United through a travel agency's tools.

Business Traveller adds the practical consequences. United says it can cancel a reservation made or changed in breach of the policy without telling the passenger. United will honour bookings an agent made before 11 November, and tells passengers to look those over for mistakes. The ban also covers paid seats and upgrades, so it follows you past the first purchase.

Why does United say it did this?

The stated reasons come in two parts. First, security. United says sharing account credentials with personal AI assistants can expose sensitive information, and that the tools may cause unintended purchases, account changes or use of MileagePlus miles.

Second, regulation. United says automated actions could breach Federal Aviation Administration and Department of Transportation rules. Its example is check-in, which needs a person to acknowledge that their bags hold no banned items such as explosives.

The outside explanations are about control. Henry Harteveldt, an aviation analyst at Atmosphere Research Group, told CBS News that United does not want yet another intermediary on top of Google search, price-comparison sites and online travel agencies. He added that "Flight searches cost airlines money", so a surge of bot searches has a bill attached. Scott Kirby, United's CEO, told a Chicago event, as the Wall Street Journal reported and Skift repeats, "I don't want to create another middleman."

Can an AI still help you plan a trip?

The coverage is not fully consistent here, and that matters. Breitbart, citing the Wall Street Journal, says agents such as Meta's Muse and OpenAI's Dots can still pull up United's flights and prices but cannot finish the booking on United's website. Business Traveller likewise says researching a trip with AI remains allowed, though United's website terms require written permission before an automated agent accesses the site.

One Mile at a Time points at a gap. The written definition includes software that merely "discovers" or searches, which could sweep in tools such as award-search sites. Whether United means that, or will enforce it selectively, is the writer's open question, not something United has said.

What are other airlines doing?

The industry is split. According to Breitbart's account of the Wall Street Journal report, Southwest launched a ChatGPT plug-in that lets customers search its flights, Delta says authenticated AI assistants can complete bookings with extra safeguards on sensitive trips, and American says some agents can already buy tickets through certain partners. The same account says none of them has moved against AI agents in public.

Outside aviation the pattern repeats. Reuters, via The Star, notes that Amazon has blocked Meta's Muse agent while Walmart partnered with it, and that Expedia and Booking are working with OpenAI. Digital Trends adds that Amazon and several major banks have also restricted unauthorised agents.

The demand side looks thin so far. Breitbart relays United's own view that it is not seeing many bookings come through AI agents at all, and says its systems can already tell whether a booking came from an individual, a travel agency or an agent. It also relays Uber's figure for Halloween week: 320 million trips across Uber and Uber Eats, of which AI agents accounted for 500 Eats orders and four rides.

What do we not know yet?

A few things are unclear. The coverage I read does not say how often United will cancel tickets or how reliably it can spot an agent working through an ordinary browser session, though Breitbart relays United saying its systems can already tell an individual, a travel agency and an agent apart. Nothing I found says whether the rule is applied differently to tickets sold outside the U.S., including in India. Harteveldt expects United fares to stay bookable through aggregators such as Kayak, Hopper and Google Flights, so finding fares should not get harder (though United itself says it will restrict access indirectly via agencies and aggregators), though blocking bots will not make them easier to find either.

Air India offers a contrast. Business Traveller notes that its eZ Booking lets a customer type or speak a request, review the itinerary and then pay, but the tool does not execute the payment itself, which is the step United is ultimately trying to block.

What should you do?

If you fly United and use an AI assistant for travel, the safe reading is simple: let the assistant research and compare, then book on United's own site or app yourself. Do not give an agent your MileagePlus login. If an agent booked something before 11 November, check the reservation.

What to watch

Watch for a second major airline copying the wording, for a travel agency or aggregator that publicly stops offering agent tools, and for any real case of United cancelling a ticket under the rule. Also watch whether OpenAI or Meta respond, since both are pushing travel as a use case.

My take

I read this as two arguments sharing one rule. The security worry is reasonable for an agent holding your login, and a ban on shared credentials would have made sense on its own. On the federal rules, Business Traveller says they do not prohibit AI bookings and that United's restriction is a separate company policy. So my reading is that banning agents even when you approve every step is as much about who owns the customer as about safety, though United has not said so. For a small business with a travel desk, I'd keep research on AI and keep the final click human on every airline, for now.

Frequently asked questions

Has United Airlines banned AI agents from booking flights?

Yes. On 11 November 2026 United added a ban on AI agents to its contract of carriage. They cannot make, change or service tickets, or buy extras such as seats and upgrades, even when the passenger supervises or approves each step. United can cancel bookings made against the rule without telling the passenger.

Why did United ban AI agents?

United says sharing account credentials with third-party assistants can expose sensitive information and cause unintended purchases, account changes or MileagePlus spending. It also says automated actions could breach FAA and DOT rules, for example the check-in confirmation about banned items. Analysts add that United wants to avoid another intermediary and the cost of bot searches.

Can I still use ChatGPT or another AI to research United flights?

Reports differ in detail. Breitbart, citing the Wall Street Journal, says agents can still show United flights and prices but cannot finish the booking. Business Traveller says research stays allowed, though United's website terms need written permission for automated access. The contract wording also covers tools that search, so it is not fully settled.

What happens to bookings an AI agent made before the ban?

Business Traveller reports that United will honour bookings made by AI agents before 11 November 2026, and tells passengers to check them for errors. It says it will add account-security advice and help with unwanted transactions to its Digital Trust Center. Bookings made against the rule after that date can be cancelled without notice.

Do other airlines allow AI agents to book?

Practice varies. Breitbart, relaying the Wall Street Journal, says Southwest has a ChatGPT plug-in for flight search, Delta allows authenticated assistants to book with extra safeguards, and American says some agents can buy through partners. Digital Trends says Delta reportedly permits authenticated assistants. None of those airlines had publicly moved against agents at the time.
