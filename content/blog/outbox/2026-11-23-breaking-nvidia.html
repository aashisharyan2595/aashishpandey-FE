Quick answer. On Monday 23 November 2026 Nvidia's board voted unanimously to reinstate Jensen Huang as president, CEO and board member, effective immediately. The investigation into the foreign intelligence reports continues.

What happened

On Monday, 23 November 2026, Nvidia announced that Jensen Huang is president, chief executive and a board member again, effective immediately. The board voted unanimously. Huang resigned on 12 November, so he was out for 11 days.

Colette Kress, who was interim CEO, keeps her role as chief financial officer. Harvey C. Jones stays on as board chair.

Why Huang left, and what the board says now

Huang resigned after disclosing "personal behavior inconsistent with the company's code of conduct," Jones said on 17 November, according to NPR. News reports tied him to a woman US intelligence officials say was recruited by China's spy agency, and to a second woman who is a Nvidia executive. NPR said it had not independently confirmed those reports. Her former employer, Megaspeed, told NPR it conducts legitimate and lawful business.

Nvidia's statement says the board held "a series of discussions and meetings" with Huang and was "satisfied with respect to Mr. Huang's candor and ability to lead." It also says the board took input from "senior levels of the United States government" and is confident, based on that input, that his return "poses no national security risk."

This is a sharp turn. On the company's earnings call on 17 November, Jones had said the board did not intend to reinstate Huang, NPR reported. TechCrunch says Nvidia had even hired an executive search firm, and that a group of tech executives, including Elon Musk, had signed a petition asking for his return.

President Trump endorsed the move on Truth Social. He said the Attorney General had confirmed the government has no evidence Huang broke the law. TechCrunch notes the Attorney General has not yet made a formal statement, and calls it an unusual claim for a president to make, since the Attorney General normally investigates independently. Trump also said Nvidia should continue its internal export-control investigation.

What is confirmed and what is not

Confirmed by Nvidia: the reinstatement, the unanimous vote, and that the independent-director committee investigating reports that Huang was the target of a foreign intelligence effort will "proceed to conclusion." Nvidia says Huang supports it and is committed to cooperating.

The board also set up a National Security and Export Control Committee of independent directors, and has started a search for a new Chief Compliance Officer. Nvidia says it will keep cooperating with government investigations.

Not settled: the investigation's findings. NPR reported last week that Huang and Nvidia's general counsel are due to answer questions behind closed doors from House committees on 1 and 2 December, about alleged chip smuggling and export-control evasion.

What the market did

TechCrunch reported Nvidia shares opened about 30% higher on Monday, and were up 27%, at $159.95, by 9:51 am ET. NPR reported on 17 November that the stock had dropped more than 16% since early November, after Huang disclosed the affair.

What to watch

The committee's findings, the December hearings, and who fills the compliance role. The export-control questions have not gone away.

My take

The board changed course within a week, with the government's input cited as a reason. I'd treat the company's confidence as a statement, not a finding, and wait for the investigation before drawing conclusions.
