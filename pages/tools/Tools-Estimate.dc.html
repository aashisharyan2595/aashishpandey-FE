How to estimate a project

  1. 1

    Start from a template or a blank list

    Pick the nearest template and change it, or add your own tasks. Give each task a best, likely and worst case in hours.

  2. 2

    Set rates and extras

    Enter an hourly rate for each role, then overhead, a risk buffer, a discount and tax if you need them. Say how many people work in parallel.

  3. 3

    Read the quote and share it

    The total, the range and the timeline update as you type. Copy the summary, download a CSV, print it or send the link.

Single-number estimates fail because every task has a range, and the risky ones are the ones people round down. This calculator asks for a best, likely and worst case for each task, turns them into an expected figure using the standard three-point (PERT) method, and widens the total when your tasks are uncertain. That gives you a quote you can defend and a safe figure to commit to.

What it does

  • Each task gets a best, likely and worst case. The expected hours are (best + 4 x likely + worst) / 6.
  • The spread between best and worst sets how much margin the 80, 90 or 95 percent figure adds.
  • Hourly rates by role, project overhead, a risk buffer, discount and tax (GST or VAT).
  • Timeline from the number of people and their productive hours a week.
  • Starting points for a marketing website, a Shopify store, a web app MVP and a mobile app.
  • Copy the summary, download a CSV, print to PDF or copy a link that restores the same estimate.

Project Estimate Calculator: questions and answers

What is a three-point estimate?

You give each task three numbers: a best case, a likely case and a worst case. The calculator weights them as (best + 4 x likely + worst) / 6. It is the PERT method, and it copes better with uncertainty than a single guess.

What does the confidence level do?

The expected total is a 50 percent figure: it is as likely to be beaten as missed. Choosing 80 or 90 percent adds a margin based on how wide your best and worst numbers are, so the quote is one you can meet more often. For most client quotes 80 percent is a sensible choice.

Should I also add a risk buffer?

The higher confidence levels already add margin, so use one or the other, not both at full strength. Keep the buffer at 0 if you quote at 80 percent or above, or use a buffer with the 50 percent expected figure.

How is the timeline worked out?

It divides the safe hours by the number of people times the productive hours each of them gets through in a week. Thirty hours is a realistic default, since meetings and admin take the rest. Dependencies between tasks are not modelled, so treat it as the shortest sensible timeline.

Are my numbers sent anywhere?

No. Everything is calculated in your browser and saved in your own browser storage. A shared link carries the estimate in the part after the # sign, which is not sent to a server when someone opens it.

How accurate are the templates?

They are starting points based on typical small projects, not promises. Change the tasks and hours to match your scope, your team and your client. The rates are placeholders for each currency, so set your own.
