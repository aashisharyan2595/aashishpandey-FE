    How to read it

    Read across a row to see who is involved in a task. Read down a column to see what one role carries. The dot beside each task turns amber or red when a rule is broken, and the workload row turns red when a role holds more than 60% of the tasks.

    Select a check to jump to the row or column it is about. Everything you change is saved in this browser as you go.

    How to create a RACI matrix

    1. 1

      Pick a template or start blank

      Choose a starting matrix such as software release or website launch, or start from a blank grid.

    2. 2

      Name your roles and tasks

      Edit the role headers and task names. Add rows and columns, and reorder them from the menu on each one.

    3. 3

      Assign R, A, C and I

      Click a cell to cycle through the letters, or select it and press R, A, C or I. Arrow keys move around the grid.

    4. 4

      Fix what the checks find

      Read the checks panel: one Accountable per task, someone Responsible, and no overloaded role.

    5. 5

      Export or share

      Download CSV or PNG, print to PDF, copy the table for a sheet, or copy a link to the matrix.

    A RACI matrix answers one question for every task: who does what? It stops two people owning the same decision and stops important work falling between roles. This builder gives you the grid, the rules and the exports in one place, without a spreadsheet or an account.

    What it does

    • Five ready templates: software release, website launch, marketing campaign, employee onboarding and event planning
    • Live checks for no Accountable, several Accountable, nobody Responsible, too many Consulted and overloaded roles
    • Click to cycle or type R, A, C, I with full keyboard control and A/R in one cell
    • Workload row that shows how much each role carries
    • Optional due date or note column
    • Undo and redo, autosave in your browser, shareable link
    • Export to CSV, PNG, print to PDF or copy to a spreadsheet
    • Nothing uploaded: runs in the browser

    Limits, honestly

    • A RACI matrix says who is involved, not when or how well. Add dates in the note column and review the matrix when people or scope change.
    • The checks follow common practice (one Accountable per task, at least one Responsible), but your organisation may use its own rules. Treat them as prompts.
    • Consulted versus Informed is a promise to actually ask people. A C that is never asked is worse than an I.
    • For fast-moving decisions, a decision framework such as DACI can fit better. You can still capture it here with a note.
    • The matrix lives in your browser. Clearing site data removes it, so export a CSV or copy a link for anything you need to keep.

    RACI Matrix Builder: questions and answers

    What is a RACI matrix?

    A RACI matrix is a grid that lists tasks down one side and roles across the top. Each cell says how that role relates to the task: Responsible (does the work), Accountable (owns the outcome and signs it off), Consulted (gives input before) or Informed (told afterwards). It removes guesswork about who does what.

    How many Accountable people can a task have?

    Exactly one. If two people are Accountable, each can assume the other will make the call. The builder flags any task with no Accountable or with more than one, so you can fix it before the matrix is shared.

    Can the same person be Responsible and Accountable?

    Yes. On small tasks the owner often does the work too, so the cell is marked A/R. Press R and A on the same cell, or click until it shows A/R. On large tasks, separating the two gives a useful check on quality.

    How many people should be Consulted?

    As few as the task needs. Every Consulted role is a two-way conversation that adds time. The builder warns when a task has more than four, because that usually means some of those people only need to be Informed.

    What is the difference between RACI, RASCI and DACI?

    RASCI adds a Support role for people who help the Responsible person. DACI replaces Responsible with Driver and Approver and suits decisions rather than delivery. RACI is the simplest and works for most projects and processes. For decision-heavy work you can still use it and add a note column.

    Can I export a RACI matrix to Excel or Google Sheets?

    Yes. Download a CSV that opens in Excel and Google Sheets, or press Copy table and paste straight into a sheet. You can also save a PNG for slides or print the matrix to PDF.

    Is my matrix saved or uploaded anywhere?

    No upload happens. The matrix is saved in your own browser so it is there when you come back, and you can undo changes. To share it, copy a link: the whole matrix is inside the link itself, not on a server.
